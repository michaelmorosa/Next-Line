# Next Line grading guidelines

Draft. Edit this in the admin (Trails → Grading guidelines). Everyone who grades features works from this page.

## How grades work

- Every feature gets a grade from **1 to 10 within its type**. A grade 5 drop and a grade 5 corner aren't meant
  to be equally hard. A rider who cleans grade 4 drops should find a grade 5 drop the next small step up.
- **Grade what it takes to ride it cleanly at normal trail speed**, in dry conditions, on a typical trail bike.
  Don't grade it by the worst line or by what a pro makes look easy.
- **Steps are small.** If two features feel more than one step apart and nothing sits between them, say so in
  the bulk trail view so we can find a feature that fills the gap.
- **Consequence counts.** A feature with a bad outcome if you get it wrong (exposure, a tree at the exit, a
  flat landing) grades higher than the same size with a safe runout. Describe the consequence in "What can go
  wrong".
- **Grade 8 and up** are features most riders shouldn't try without coaching or a lot of experience. Riders
  only get these suggested after turning on "Advanced features".
- Unsure between two grades? Pick the higher one and leave a note for the second reviewer.

## Certifying

- Whoever adds a feature can't be the one who certifies it. A second team member rides or looks at it, checks
  the grade against this page, and certifies it.
- Re-check a feature at least once a year (Mark verified), and flag it **changed** after rebuilds or storms.

## Drops (height from takeoff lip to landing)

| Grade | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Roll-down under 12 in, can be rolled at walking pace |
| 2 | 12–20 in roll-off, sloped landing |
| 3 | 20–28 in, rollable, sloped landing |
| 4 | 28–36 in, needs a little speed and a manual or lift, sloped landing |
| 5 | 3–4 ft, not rollable, good landing |
| 6 | 4–5 ft, or smaller to flat |
| 7 | 5–6½ ft, committing |
| 8 | 6½–8 ft, or 5 ft+ to flat |
| 9 | 8–11 ft |
| 10 | Over 11 ft or very high consequence |

## Jumps (gap from lip to knuckle, tables count as half)

| Grade | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Rollers and small tables you can roll |
| 2 | Small tabletops, under 6 ft |
| 3 | Tabletops 6–10 ft |
| 4 | Tables 10–13 ft, or small rollable gaps |
| 5 | Gaps up to 10 ft with a soft knuckle |
| 6 | Gaps 10–16 ft |
| 7 | Gaps 16–23 ft, or step-ups and step-downs that need precise speed |
| 8 | Gaps 23–33 ft |
| 9 | Gaps 33–43 ft |
| 10 | Bigger, or high consequence |

## Corners

1 wide flat turn · 2 tight flat turn · 3 small berm · 4 big or fast berm · 5 loose or off-camber turn ·
6 tight switchback · 7 steep switchback needing a nose pivot or a hop · 8 exposed switchback ·
9 loose exposed switchback on a steep · 10 extreme exposure.

## Rock gardens and roots

1 a few scattered rocks or roots · 2 a short rough patch · 3 a longer rough patch with one clear line ·
4 line choice matters, some ledges · 5 continuous chunk with ledges up to 12 in · 6 large rocks, needs speed
and lifts · 7 big ledges, holes or wet roots that punish the wrong line · 8–10 the hardest chunk around:
big moves back to back with consequences.

## Steeps (average slope of the steep part)

1 under 15° · 2 15–20° · 3 20–25° · 4 25–30° · 5 30–33° with a good runout · 6 33–36° ·
7 over 36°, or steep with a tight exit · 8 steep with a hard move on it · 9 near-vertical rolls ·
10 extreme.

## Skinnies (width and height above ground)

1 wide and low · 2 12 in wide, low · 3 8 in wide, low · 4 8 in, waist high or with a turn · 5 6 in, low ·
6 6 in, high or with a turn · 7 4 in · 8–10 narrow, high, and with drops or turns.

## Climbs (technical moves going up)

1 smooth short pitch · 2 steeper pitch · 3 loose or rooty pitch · 4 a ledge up to 6 in · 5 ledges up to
10 in, or a long steep loose pitch · 6 ledges 10–14 in · 7 linked ledges, tight turns on a steep ·
8–10 the hardest tech climbs, needing ratcheting, hops and precise balance.
